\paragraph{Extended learning diagnostics.}
The probes connect reward design to observable selection and interaction behavior. Public-reward selection yields mean hidden quality 0.20 versus 0.075 under uniform random selection, with gains concentrated in A. Five completed feedback/view controls reveal useful experimental axes: GPT solves B-L2-s0 with execution-only feedback where its structured/scalar runs do not; fixed-view C-L2-s0 succeeds in 10 calls versus 26 with active access. These exploratory cases guide larger matched panels; one incomplete Claude C continuation is excluded.

Eight matched-budget arms compare independent proposals with public-feedback refinement across C, D, two-operation A and E, using three proposals per stage. A public-only JSON adapter supplies executable formatting without target answers. Native scoring accepts 26 of 30 proposals, allowing the benchmark to isolate geometric planning from interface competence. With strict completion at 0/8 and zero admissible reward for 29 proposals, this cohort identifies where staged curricula and denser progress feedback can be evaluated against unchanged acceptance criteria.

Two D studies expose actual shaped reward, each reusing a starter and adding two generations: the matched first proposal or the model's first publicly executable proposal. Both retain their starting reward and finish below strict completion. A separate safe D candidate earns partial reward 0.4 with transfer still incomplete, illustrating the distinction between admissible progress and end-to-end success. These probes establish auditable starting points for curriculum and RL studies, with expanded training and held-out evaluation to measure policy gains.
